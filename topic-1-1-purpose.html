    Section 1.1 Purpose and Behavior

    45 minutes
    🔗

    Subsection 1.1.1 Learning Objectives

    Learning Objective 1.1.1. 1.1.A.

    Determine the purpose and behavior of an app by interacting with it.
    🔗
    🔗

    Learning Objective 1.1.2. 1.1.B.

    Identify inputs and outputs of an app while using it.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    Subsection 1.1.2 Purpose and Behavior of Apps

    Essential Knowledge 1.1.3. 1.1.A.1.

    Applications (apps) are programs that run on many kinds of computing devices. Apps can run on smartphones, tablets, laptops, desktops, game consoles, and web browsers, and they are designed to help users accomplish tasks or goals.
    🔗
    🔗

    Essential Knowledge 1.1.4. 1.1.A.2.

    People use apps to help solve real problems and accomplish everyday tasks, such as communicating, learning, navigating, creating and sharing media, staying organized, and being entertained.
    🔗
    🔗

    Essential Knowledge 1.1.5. 1.1.A.3.

    An app is successful when it uses information to produce an output that helps a user achieve a goal. An app’s value to the user depends on how well the results meet their needs.
    🔗
    🔗

    Essential Knowledge 1.1.6. 1.1.A.4.

    Because people rely on apps for everyday tasks, app behavior matters. When an app is confusing, unreliable, or does not work as expected, users can become frustrated because it interrupts what they are trying to do.
    🔗
    🔗

    Essential Knowledge 1.1.7. 1.1.A.5.

    An app’s behavior is what it does while it is running, including how it responds to user interactions. The behavior of an app is determined by its program code.
    🔗
    🔗

    Essential Knowledge 1.1.8. 1.1.A.6.

    An app should behave as intended across a range of conditions, including different ways people may interact with it.
    🔗
    🔗

    Essential Knowledge 1.1.9. 1.1.A.7.

    Users can understand an app’s purpose and behavior by using it and observing how it responds to their actions.
    🔗
    🔗
    🔗

    Subsection 1.1.3 Inputs and Outputs

    Essential Knowledge 1.1.10. 1.1.B.1.

    Apps are designed to take in information from a variety of sources, process that information, and respond in useful ways.
    🔗
    🔗

    Essential Knowledge 1.1.11. 1.1.B.2.

    When users interact with apps, they often tap or click buttons, type letters or numbers, speak, or move the device. Each of these actions provides input: data sent to a computer for a program to process.
    🔗
    🔗

    Essential Knowledge 1.1.12. 1.1.B.3.

    Apps should provide clear feedback when they receive input, such as highlighting a button, showing a loading icon, playing a sound, vibrating, or displaying an error message. This feedback is a form of output that helps users know the app received the input, is working, and what step to take next.
    🔗
    🔗

    Essential Knowledge 1.1.13. 1.1.B.4.

    Input often comes from a user’s direct actions, but it can also be captured automatically from sources such as sensors (like an accelerometer), location, time, or network signals. Apps may also receive audio or images from microphones and cameras, often through user actions (like a voice command or face scan). These inputs allow apps to provide features like maps, step counting, face filters, or recommendations.
    🔗
    🔗

    Essential Knowledge 1.1.14. 1.1.B.5.

    In many apps, the program responds to events, such as button clicks, key presses, or sensor updates, by running specific code. Events are one way input is delivered to a program. An event signals that something happened, and an event handler processes the input and determines what happens next.
    🔗
    🔗

    Essential Knowledge 1.1.15. 1.1.B.6.

    Outputs are what an app produces. Outputs may include text, images, sounds, or something the user feels, like a vibration. Some outputs are directly perceived by the user, while others are not, such as data written to a file or sent over a network.
    🔗
    🔗

    Essential Knowledge 1.1.16. 1.1.B.7.

    Users can identify inputs and outputs by trying different actions in an app and observing what the app displays or does in response.
